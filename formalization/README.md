# Lean formalization

A Lean 4 formalization with Mathlib of the mathematics in "Exact Sampling from Neural Networks:
Depth, Attention, and Decoding". The library `ExactSampling` has 63 modules and
2,413 theorems. It builds with warnings treated as errors, and every one of its
3,412 declarations depends only on the standard axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and
`Quot.sound`.

## Checking it

```sh
lake exe cache get    # Mathlib build cache for the pinned commit
python3 verify.py     # build, axiom audit, paper-label check; writes lean-verification.json
```

Toolchain: Lean `v4.34.0-rc2`, Mathlib `2631d1cc8c2ace6c6a900425d6e5d2b5963966e9`.
`verify.py` rejects `sorry`, `admit`, `axiom`, `native_decide`, `implemented_by`, `unsafe`,
`opaque`, `@[extern]` and unbounded heartbeats; checks that the root file imports every module;
builds the library; runs `AxiomAudit.lean`, which enumerates every declaration of the library
by module (so nothing can be skipped) and prints its axioms; and checks that every paper label
cited in a docstring exists in a TeX file compiled into one of the two PDFs.

## How the formalization relates to the paper

Each theorem's docstring names the paper result it formalizes by its TeX label (for example
`lem:conf-replay`), never by number, and says whether it is the statement itself or a step of the
proof. `PAPER_MAP.md` lists, for each of the 329 cited labels, the theorems citing it, in the
order the labels appear in the paper. It is generated by `../tools/lean_map.py`.

Every module begins with a header that lists what it formalizes and, explicitly, what it does
not. Three kinds of gap recur:

- **Cost model.** Bit-operation counts, data structures, certified arithmetic routines (interval
  division, square-root bisection, series evaluation) and running times are not formalized.
  Quantities such as the per-token work `G(p + log(T+2))` enter as functions with the paper's
  bounds as hypotheses.
- **Stand-in hypotheses.** Where a proof imports a fact from outside the paper's own arguments,
  such as a coefficient bound from complex analysis, the cosh product of DLMF 4.36.2, or the
  identification of a counting quantity with an integral formula, the Lean statement takes that
  fact as a named hypothesis. Each one is listed in the module header and in the docstring.
  Hypotheses are never equivalent to the conclusion; several were removed in review when they
  were.
- **Probability model.** Exactness of a sampler is stated as an identity of probabilities or
  expectations. The conference modules and the replay lemma use genuine measures (Lebesgue
  measure on `[0,1)`, `Measure.pi` for fresh uniform bits, independent trials via `iIndepFun`).
  The lower bounds are proved for samplers given as finite mixtures of decision trees with cached
  repeat queries, and the expected cost is the expected number of distinct probes. The reduction
  from an almost surely terminating algorithm with an infinite random tape to such a mixture is
  not formalized.

## Modules

| Directory | Paper | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| `Conference/` | conference version, Sections 3 to 7 | One bounded tanh row (`prop:conf-row`): Poisson race law, tanh mean, renewal cost, finite-bit Poisson sampler; repeated independent trials; bounded rational charts (`lem:conf-chart`); the appendable grid index and its exact rejection sampler with the `17/8` proposal bound (`thm:conf-grid`); the search obstruction; the finite moment evaluator (`lem:conf-moments`); depth-uniform prefix stability with layer maps on prefix arrays (`lem:conf-stability`); exact sampling from maintained evaluation, the undecided tail, the autoregressive joint law, the background schedule and the expected work (`lem:conf-replay`); the fixed-model corollary. |
| `Tanh/` | Appendix A.1 to A.9 | The scalar factory, interval invariant, races and offspring bounds; radius recursions, rounding, call counts and the sharp rates; majority expansion, arity bounds and the one-row request bound; product-arity laws and samplers; stopped work and lazy comparisons; the bounded softmax head. |
| `Lower/` | `lem:transcript`, `thm:lower` and related | Transcript and multivariate information inequalities, coupling, the lower bounds of `thm:lower`, the finite joint and finite critical laws, windows, evaluation separation, attention position lower bounds, integer scores. |
| `Instance/` | Appendix D | Exact Boolean gates in `tanh`, the SAT-parity constructions at norm eight and at norm `s`, weight obstructions, score certificates, and the flow program of `lem:instanceflow`. |
| `Critical/` | Appendix A.15 to A.23 | Critical lemmas and the score ceiling, the signed strip and bottlenecks, corrected Bernstein telescopes, fixed-rank batching arithmetic, and the column-envelope construction with its tilting laws. |
| `Residual/` | Appendix A.10 to A.14 | Residual networks: interval invariants, thinning, radius and range bounds, potentials, the fused factory and the zero-bias counting chain. |
| `Stopping/` | stopping lemmas | The cached-residual exactness identity, Tonelli bounds for stopped work, geometric precision tails, and the comparison and replay cost sums. |
| `Attention/` | Appendix B | The append-only index at fixed rank, certified replay, the moment decoder, finite query lists, the implicit rank-one query, the indexed lower-bound gadgets, and attention heads. |
| `Normalization/` | Appendix C | RMS floors, the context law of unstabilized normalization, Gaussian RMS factories, scalar RMS laws, pre-norm additive composition and lower bounds, normalized gains, and feed-forward blocks. |
| `Checkpoint/` | Appendix E | Checkpoint certificates: RMS Lipschitz bounds, precision guards, score rank, rotary spans, key residuals, initial radii and the worksheet counts. |
| `Links/` | across modules | Composed statements in which a stand-in hypothesis of one module is discharged by a theorem proved in another. |

Some results appear twice: once in `Conference/` in the form the conference version states them,
and once in a full-version module. The full-version modules point to the stronger conference
forms in their headers.

## Files

- `ExactSampling.lean`: root file importing every module.
- `AxiomAudit.lean`: enumerates every declaration of the library and prints its axioms.
- `verify.py`: the checks above; writes `lean-verification.json`.
- `lean-verification.json`: the last verification record (theorem list, axioms, label map).
- `PAPER_MAP.md`: paper label to Lean theorems.
