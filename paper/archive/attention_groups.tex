\subsection{Exact sampling from an indexed cache with a small cover}
\label{sec:newcover}

Let $k_1,\ldots,k_T\in[-S,S]^n$ be cached keys, let
$v_1,\ldots,v_T\in[-1,1]$ be one coordinate of the cached values,
and let $q\in[-S,S]^n$ be a query. Here $S>0$ and $\beta\ge0$.
Write
\[
 a_j(q)=\frac\beta n\langle q,k_j\rangle,
 \qquad Z(q)=\sum_{j=1}^T e^{a_j(q)},
 \qquad Y(q)=\frac{\sum_j e^{a_j(q)}v_j}{Z(q)}.
\]
A sample from attention means either an index $J$ with
$\Pr(J=j)=e^{a_j}/Z$, or a sign whose mean is $Y$.
The second follows from the first by a fresh sign of mean $v_J$.
All cached coordinates in the explicit-cache model are finite dyadic
numbers. The standard scaling $\beta=\sqrt n$ is allowed as a
computable algebraic parameter.

\begin{lemma}[A partition certificate for exact attention]
\label{lem:newpartition}
Suppose prefill partitions $[T]$ into nonempty groups
$G_1,\ldots,G_M$, stores their sizes $N_g$, and chooses representatives
$c_g\in[-S,S]^n$. Suppose a known number $\delta>0$ satisfies
\[
 \left|\frac\beta n\langle q,k_j-c_g\rangle\right|\le\delta
 \quad(j\in G_g)
\]
for every allowed query. Put $b_g(q)=\beta\langle q,c_g\rangle/n$.
The following procedure samples attention exactly:
choose $g$ with probability proportional to $N_g e^{b_g(q)}$,
choose $j$ uniformly in $G_g$, and accept it with probability
$e^{a_j(q)-b_g(q)-\delta}$; repeat after rejection.
It uses at most $e^{2\delta}$ proposals in expectation.
\end{lemma}
\paragraph{Proof idea.}
The group proposal and the within-group uniform choice cancel the group size.  The final exponential correction leaves exactly the desired member weight, while the certificate bounds the rejection loss.

\begin{proof}
The probability of proposing and accepting a particular $j\in G_g$
in a trial is
\[
 \frac{N_ge^{b_g}}{\sum_hN_he^{b_h}}\frac1{N_g}
 e^{a_j-b_g-\delta}
 =\frac{e^{a_j-\delta}}{\sum_hN_he^{b_h}}.
\]
Conditioning on success gives the asserted index law. Moreover,
$e^{-\delta}\sum_gN_ge^{b_g}\le Z\le
e^{\delta}\sum_gN_ge^{b_g}$, so the success probability is at
least $e^{-2\delta}$. Independent trials have geometric length.
The requested value sign is fresh after acceptance, which gives its
mean by conditioning on the accepted index.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[An explicit query and a finite cache]
\label{thm:newexplicitcover}
Fix a partition as above. Suppose all explicit coordinates, $S$,
and $\delta$ are dyadic of encoding length at most $b$, and
$\beta$ is either such a dyadic or $\sqrt n$.
If $q$ is explicitly supplied, there is an exact finite-bit sampler
using at most $(9/8)e^{2\delta}$ proposals in expectation. For
\[
 B\ge 8+b+\lceil\log_2(T+n+2)\rceil,
\]
its expected online work is
\[
 O\bigl((nM+ne^{2\delta})B^4\bigr).
\]
The $nM$ term is setup for the query; it can be shared by repeated
samples from that same query. There is no further dependence on
the numerical magnitude of the scores. Prefill stores the group
members in contiguous arrays.
\end{theorem}
\paragraph{Proof idea.}
Compute one representative score per group, then replace its exponential by a certified dyadic upper bound.  The total added mass is small, and the final correction uses the actual selected member score.

\begin{proof}
Compute the $M$ representative inner products, and find their
largest score $b_*$. Since the temperature is nonnegative, the
comparisons use exact dyadic inner products even when
$\beta=\sqrt n$. Put
\[
 w_g=e^{b_g-b_*}\le1,\qquad W=\sum_gN_gw_g\ge1,
 \qquad P=\lceil\log_2(16T)\rceil.
\]
The negative-exponential routine in
Lemma~\ref{lem:newnegativeexp} returns dyadic upper bounds $u_g$
with
\[
 2^{-P}\le u_g,\qquad w_g\le u_g\le w_g+2^{1-P}.
\]
Obtain them by adding $2^{-P}$ to the upper endpoints of enclosures
of width $2^{-P}$. Exact integer prefix sums for the weights
$N_gu_g$ sample a group; then choose a uniform member $j$.
For that member accept with probability
\[
 \frac{\exp(a_j-b_*-\delta)}{u_g}.
\]
The certificate gives $a_j-b_*-\delta\le b_g-b_*\le0$, and
the acceptance probability is at most one. Writing
$W'=\sum_gN_gu_g$, we have
\[
 W'\le W+T2^{1-P}\le W+\tfrac18\le\tfrac98W.
\]
The accepted mass of $j$ is $e^{a_j-b_*-\delta}/W'$. Therefore
its conditional law is exact, and the mean number of proposals is
\[
 \frac{W'e^{b_*+\delta}}{Z}
 \le\frac98\frac{e^\delta\sum_gN_ge^{b_g}}Z
 \le\frac98e^{2\delta}.
\]

All stored proposal weights have $O(P)$ bits after a common dyadic
scaling. The prefix data structure takes $O(MP)$ bit operations to
construct and samples a group with $O(P\log(M+1))$ bit operations
and $O(P)$ random bits in expectation. A uniform group member has
$O(\log T)$ bit cost. To implement its acceptance coin at precision
$t$, evaluate the nonpositive exponential to precision $P+t+3$,
since $u_g\ge2^{-P}$. Its exponent is a rational plus a rational
divided by $\sqrt n$ in the standard-scaled case. The proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:newnegativeexp} applies to that form as well.
It costs $O(n(B+t)^4)$ including the fresh inner product.
A lazy-uniform comparison has refinement tail at most $2^{2-t}$.
Summing stage costs against that tail and multiplying by the
proposal bound gives the asserted expected work. Only
$M$ representative inner products are needed for setup.
\end{proof}

The next lemma records a classical ingredient with explicit source
counts. It is the polynomial logistic race of Dughmi, Hartline,
Kleinberg, and Niazadeh, specialized to an integer temperature.
Its proof is included to specify the interface used by the index.

\begin{lemma}[Polynomial logistic race]
\label{lem:newpolyrace}
Let $m\ge1$ and let $\lambda\ge8$ be an integer. Given independent
access to coins of unknown biases $p_1,\ldots,p_m\in[0,1]$, one can
sample an index with law proportional to $e^{\lambda p_i}$ using at
most
\[
 C\lambda^4m^2\log(4m\lambda)
\]
input-coin calls in expectation, for an absolute constant $C$.
The construction uses only integer
or rational bookkeeping, a scalar linear Bernoulli factory with
known slack, and Poisson coins of a rational parameter. It has
finite expected bit work polynomial in $m$, $\lambda$, and the
source-address length.
\end{lemma}
\paragraph{Proof idea.}
An empirical maximum gives a coin near the largest unknown bias.  Subtracting that estimate through a linear factory creates uniformly favorable acceptance probabilities while preserving all relative exponential weights.

\begin{proof}
Put $\epsilon=1/\lambda$ and choose an integer
$N\ge2\lambda^2\log(4m\lambda)$ with
$N\le2\lambda^2\log(4m\lambda)+2$.
For each $i$, take $N$ input samples and form its empirical mean
$\widehat p_i$. Let $Z$ be a coin with conditional probability
$\max_i\widehat p_i$, implemented by a uniform integer in $[N]$.
Hoeffding's inequality and a union bound show
\[
 \Pr\!\left(\max_i|\widehat p_i-p_i|>\epsilon/2\right)
 \le2m e^{-N\epsilon^2/2}\le\epsilon/2.
\]
Consequently the unknown bias $z$ of $Z$ satisfies
$|z-p_{\max}|\le\epsilon$. Every call for a $Z$ coin uses a fresh
empirical experiment.

For each $i$, a fair choice between a $p_i$ coin and a complement
of a $Z$ coin gives a coin of bias $(p_i+1-z)/2$. Apply the linear
factory with multiplier $C=2(1-2\epsilon)$ to obtain a coin of bias
\[
 p'_i=(1-2\epsilon)(p_i+1-z).
\]
It has slack at least $\epsilon$ because
$p'_i\le(1-2\epsilon)(1+\epsilon)\le1-\epsilon$.
Huber's linear-factory bound $9.5C/\epsilon$ gives at most
$19\lambda$ mixture-coin calls. The largest new bias is at least
$1-3\epsilon$.

Run a uniform Bernoulli race on the acceptance coins
$\exp[-\lambda'(1-p'_i)]$, where
$\lambda'=\lambda/(1-2\epsilon)\le4\lambda/3$.
Each acceptance coin is a Poisson product of $p'_i$ coins.
Since $\lambda'(1-p'_{\max})\le4$, a trial succeeds with
probability at least $e^{-4}/m$. The accepted law is correct because
$\lambda'p'_i=\lambda p_i+\lambda(1-z)$, with a common second term.
Even without early termination of the Poisson product, there are at
most $(4/3)e^4\lambda m$ calls to the $p'_i$ factories.
Each costs at most $(19/2)\lambda(1+mN)$ original coin calls.
Since $N+1\le3\lambda^2\log(4m\lambda)$, their product is at most
$38e^4\lambda^4m^2\log(4m\lambda)$, so $C=4096$ suffices.
The case $m=1$ can simply return its sole index.

The sample counts, empirical maximum, uniform integer, and rational
parameters have polynomial bookkeeping cost. Huber's construction
with rational multiplier and slack has polynomial expected bit
cost; the finite-bit implementation used for the normalization
factory applies here with inverse slack $\lambda$.
Poisson parameters are rational and at most $4\lambda/3$, and can
be split into $O(\lambda)$ bounded-parameter Poisson variables.
Certified scalar comparisons have exponentially decreasing
refinement tails as in Theorem~\ref{thm:newexplicitcover}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[An implicit query]
\label{thm:newimplicitcover}
Suppose query coordinate $q_i$ is accessible through a fresh sign
of mean $q_i/S$. Assume the stronger, query-independent certificate
\[
 \frac{\beta S}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n|k_{j,i}-c_{g,i}|\le\delta
 \qquad(j\in G_g).
\]
Let $\bar A$ be a positive integer with
$\beta S^2\le\bar A\le\beta S^2+2$, and put
$\lambda=2\bar A+\lceil\log_2T\rceil+8$.
An exact attention index can be sampled using at most
\[
 Ce^{2\delta}\lambda^4 M^2\log(4M\lambda)
       +e^{2\delta}-1
\]
query-coordinate signs in expectation, for an absolute constant $C$.
One additional fresh value
sign produces a sign of mean $Y(q)$. All auxiliary work has finite
expectation polynomial in $\lambda,M,B$ for fixed $\delta$.
\end{theorem}
\paragraph{Proof idea.}
Include group multiplicities in their proposal scores through known logarithmic coins.  A polynomial logistic race selects the group, and a stopped Poisson product corrects its representative score to the chosen member score.

\begin{proof}
The representative score $b_g$ is a signed linear combination of
query-coordinate means with sum of absolute coefficients at most
$A\le\bar A$. Thus a coin with bias $(1+b_g/\bar A)/2$ needs at
most one query-coordinate sign, using a rational alias table for the absolute representative
coordinates.  Its total coefficient mass, divided by $\bar A$,
is a known gate; a closed gate returns a fair sign.  At standard
scaling this gate can be algebraic, and is sampled by certified
intervals.  No table stores irrational probabilities exactly. A mixture with known coins gives a coin of bias
\[
 p_g=\frac{b_g+\bar A+\log N_g}{\lambda}\in[0,1].
\]
Explicitly, use the preceding unknown coin with probability
$2\bar A/\lambda$; on the other branch use a known coin of bias
$\log N_g/(\lambda-2\bar A)$. Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:newpolyrace}. Since
$e^{\lambda p_g}=e^{\bar A}N_ge^{b_g}$, it generates exactly the
group proposal of Lemma~\ref{lem:newpartition}.

The score difference $d_j=a_j-b_g$ is a signed linear combination
with coefficient mass at most $\delta$. A coin of bias
$(1+d_j/\delta)/2$ therefore costs at most one query-coordinate
sign.  As for the representatives, a rational alias table for
$|k_{j,i}-c_{g,i}|$ is combined with a known gate for its total
coefficient mass divided by $\delta$. A stopped product with Poisson parameter $2\delta$ accepts
with probability $e^{d_j-\delta}$.
The geometric proposal bound multiplies the group cost by at most
$e^{2\delta}$. The stopped-Poisson identity
\[
 g(p)=\frac{1-e^{-2\delta(1-p)}}{1-p}
 \le(e^{2\delta}-1)e^{-2\delta(1-p)}
\]
shows, after averaging over the proposed groups and members and
dividing by the trial acceptance probability, that all the
difference tests together need at most $e^{2\delta}-1$ query signs.
Fresh randomness for every recursive request proves the required
conditional independence. Known logarithmic coins are implemented
by rational enclosures and the same lazy-uniform comparison.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A cover from affine dimension]
\label{cor:newrankcover}
Suppose the cached keys have affine dimension at most $r$. For
$\beta S>0$, a greedy Euclidean cover with radius
\[
 \rho=\frac{\delta\sqrt n}{\beta S}
\]
has at most $M\le(1+2\beta S^2/\delta)^r$ representatives and
satisfies both certificates above. It can be constructed in
$O(TnM)$ distance-arithmetic operations and stored, with all alias
tables, in $O(Tn+Mn)$ numbers and $O(T)$ position addresses.
At standard scaling and fixed $r,S,\delta$, its preprocessing is
$T\operatorname{poly}(n,B)$ and its expected online work is
$\operatorname{poly}(n,B,\log T)$, including for implicit queries.
\end{corollary}
\paragraph{Proof idea.}
A Euclidean cover controls every allowed score difference by Cauchy--Schwarz.  Disjoint smaller balls in the affine span bound the number of representatives, and squared-distance comparisons make the construction exact.

\begin{proof}
Greedily make a new representative whenever a key is farther than
$\rho$ from all existing representatives. Final representatives
are $\rho$-separated, and each assigned key is within $\rho$ of
its representative. Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \frac{\beta S}{n}\|k_j-c_g\|_1
 \le\frac{\beta S}{\sqrt n}\|k_j-c_g\|_2\le\delta.
\]
All keys lie in the Euclidean ball of radius $S\sqrt n$. Projecting
the origin orthogonally onto their affine span gives a center in
that span at distance at most $S\sqrt n$ from every key. Within
the affine span, disjoint balls of radius $\rho/2$ around the
representatives lie in a ball of radius $S\sqrt n+\rho/2$.
Comparison of $r$-dimensional volumes proves the bound. If $r=0$,
there is one representative. Squared distances and $\rho^2$ are
rational for dyadic $\beta$, and also when $\beta=\sqrt n$, so
all greedy decisions are exact finite arithmetic. Alias tables for
representatives and within-group differences take linear space in
the total number of their coordinates.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Scope.}
The cover is a sufficient, verifiable certificate. No converse is
asserted. A cache can have large affine dimension and still be easy,
for example if all relevant values are equal. A nonlinear factory
that requests independent attention signs can use this index as a
subroutine. Its number of attention requests must still be included
in the total cost. This is also why the result does not assert a
depth-independent bound for an arbitrary transformer.
